// How it works

Awake while it works. Asleep when it's done.

Keep-awake timers make you guess how long the work will take, and leaving a movie playing wastes memory. Dozex watches the work itself, so your Mac sleeps on time. What Dozex checks →

1

Start the work

Export a video, start a download, prompt Claude or kick off a build. Then walk away.

2

The eye opens

Dozex notices within seconds and the menu bar eye opens. Your Mac won't idle-sleep, even if you're gone for hours.

3

It dozes off

When the last task ends, Dozex lets go and your normal sleep settings take over. A notification tells you it's done.

// Questions

Good to know

More answers in the troubleshooting guide →

Can it keep my Mac awake while Final Cut Pro exports?

Yes. Final Cut Pro is watched out of the box. Once an export or render has kept it busy for about 15 seconds, your Mac stays awake until it finishes. No changing sleep settings and no movie playing in the background.

Will an idle app or agent keep my Mac awake all night?

No. Apps, agents, editors and ssh sessions only count while they keep working, so a project left open or a session waiting at its prompt lets your Mac sleep as usual.

What happens if I close my MacBook's lid?

macOS always sleeps when the lid closes without an external display, and no app can change that without admin tweaks. Keep the lid open for long jobs, or use an external display.

What does it change on my Mac?

Nothing permanent. To stay awake it uses a standard macOS power assertion, the same one video players use. If you connect Claude Code, it adds a few hooks to ~/.claude/settings.json after backing it up, and removes them if you disconnect.

Is it free?

Yes. Dozex is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account and no analytics.
