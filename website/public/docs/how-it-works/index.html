How it works

What Dozer checks, and what it changes on your Mac.

Every 3 seconds Dozer takes a quick look at what's running. If anything counts as work, it holds a macOS power assertion; when nothing does, it releases it.

The pieces

PartWhat it does
Power assertionHolds PreventUserIdleSystemSleep, the same mechanism caffeinate and video players use, and optionally PreventUserIdleDisplaySleep.
App watcherMatches running apps to your watch list and adds up the CPU of each app and the processes it started.
Network watcherReads the byte counters of your Wi-Fi and Ethernet connections and turns them into a transfer rate.
Terminal watcherReads ps and checks the foreground job of each terminal tab.
Claude Code hooksSmall scripts that create and delete a marker file per session in ~/Library/Application Support/Sleepable/sessions.
SmoothingSomething becomes busy after 4 of the last 5 readings are above its threshold, and idle again once at most 1 is. Quick spikes and short pauses don't flip it.

What it changes on your Mac

  • Nothing permanent. Quitting Dozer releases the power assertion immediately.
  • If you connect Claude Code: hooks in ~/.claude/settings.json, after a backup. Disconnecting removes them.
  • Its own settings, stored like any other app's preferences.

Privacy

Everything runs on your Mac. Dozer doesn't collect analytics, needs no account, and only goes online to check GitHub for updates.
