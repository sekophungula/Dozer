Downloads and uploads barely use the CPU, so the app check can't see them. Dozex watches how much data your Mac's Wi-Fi and Ethernet are moving instead.

What counts

Traffic of 250 KB/s or more in either direction, kept up for about 15 seconds. That covers:

  • Big downloads in Safari, Chrome, Firefox or any other app, including download managers and game launchers.
  • Uploads to YouTube, Vimeo, Dropbox, WeTransfer or a client's server, a common last step after an export.
  • iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox syncing a big folder.
  • Online backups.

Music streaming, messages, email and small syncs are well under the threshold, and short bursts don't count.

When it lets go

Once the transfer has slowed below the threshold for about 12 seconds, it no longer counts. After the grace period your Mac can sleep.

Good to know

  • It works for every app at once, without any permission prompts. It reads the same counters as Activity Monitor's Network tab.
  • It sees your Mac's total traffic, not which app is doing it, so the menu says Downloading or Uploading with the speed.
  • A long video call or a video stream can also count. That's harmless: those apps keep your Mac awake anyway while they play.
  • VPN traffic is counted once, on your real network connection.

Turn it off with Watch downloads in the menu bar panel.
